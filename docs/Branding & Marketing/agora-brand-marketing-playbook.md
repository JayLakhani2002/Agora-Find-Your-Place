## 1. The brand in one breath

**Agora is the place international students in Berlin go to find their footing** — their community and their work. It surfaces only the jobs you're legally eligible for (visa, hours, German level), writes the tailored CV and cover letter that pass ATS, and walks you through to the interview.

The differentiator is **not** "AI tailors your CV" — that's now table stakes. The differentiator is that Agora *understands the German system*: the 140-day rule, the 20-hour cap, the Minijob threshold, BAföG impact, bilingual CV norms. That knowledge is the moat, and it's the thing the whole brand should make audible.

**Brand worldview:** the modern job search drowns international students in listings they can't take, in languages they don't speak, with CVs that don't pass the filter. Agora cuts through that. *Signal over noise — and you're not searching alone.*

## 2. What "Agora" means & why we chose it (the public FAQ)

These are ready-to-use answers. Use them on the About page, in interviews, in the build log, and when investors or students ask. Keep them consistent.

### "What does Agora mean?"

> The agora (Greek: *ἀγορά*, from *ageirein*, "to gather") was the central public square of an ancient Greek city — the marketplace *and* the gathering place. It's where citizens met, traded, found work, debated, and belonged. It was the heart of the city: the place you went to become part of it.
>
> That's what we're building. Agora is the place you arrive into, find your people, and find your work.

### "Why did you name it Agora?" (the short version)

> Because it's the one word that holds everything we do at once — community, work, and opportunity. An international student arriving in Berlin needs all three, and the agora was historically the place that offered all three. The name carries belonging in it.

### "Why Agora and not something that just says 'jobs'?" (the deeper version)

> A name like "JobApp" tells you what the product is but nothing about what it's *for*. Our differentiator isn't that we list jobs — dozens of platforms do that. It's that we help you *belong* somewhere new: the right role, your community, your footing in a foreign system. "Jobs" names a feature. "Agora" names the feeling. We chose the feeling, because that's the part no competitor can copy.

### "How do you pronounce it?"

> **AH-gor-ah.** (The Greek stresses the last syllable, *ah-go-RAH*; English speakers often say *uh-GOR-uh*. Either is fine — we say AH-gor-ah.)

### "Isn't Agora a common word? Isn't it taken?"

> It's an ancient word that belongs to everyone — which is part of why we love it. There are companies called Agora in other industries (developer tools, hospitality), but none in our space: jobs and careers for international students. We're *the* Agora for that. We hold our niche.

### A hidden detail worth telling (for press / founder story)

> The Greek word for a guide was *xenagós* — literally "one who leads strangers." (*Xenos* = foreigner, *agō* = to lead.) It's the root of the modern word for a tour guide. That's exactly what Agora is: something that leads newcomers through an unfamiliar place until they belong. Our guide character, Ari, is the embodiment of that idea.

## 3. Meet Ari — the brand ambassador

Ari is the face of Agora across every channel — the way Vodafone's ZooZoos or Duolingo's owl carry their brands. Ari is a **2D illustrated human guide**, and Ari *is* the ambassador: the friendly, knowledgeable presence who welcomes you and walks beside you.

### Who Ari is

- **Name:** Ari. Short, warm, and pronounceable across every cohort's language (sits naturally in Indian, Turkish, Brazilian, and European names alike), and gender-flexible — which itself serves belonging.
- **The hidden meaning:** Ari nods to **Ariadne**, who gave Theseus the thread to find his way out of the labyrinth. The German job-and-visa system *is* a labyrinth. Ari hands you the thread.
- **The deeper tie:** Ari is a *xenagós* — the guide who leads strangers — and a wink to **Hermes**, the Greek god of the marketplace (*Hermes Agoraios*) and protector of travellers, whose winged sandals can show up as a subtle design detail.

### What Ari looks like

- **Stylized, never photoreal.** A warm, illustrated character that can be drawn demographically *open* — read as "could be anyone, could be me." This is the deliberate fix to the risk of a single human face excluding part of a multicultural audience. A photoreal face would pick an ethnicity and an age; a stylized one belongs to everyone.
- **One quiet classical detail** ties Ari to Agora without costume kitsch: an olive-leaf pin, a subtly draped scarf, or winged-sandal laces. Pick one and keep it consistent.
- **Production note:** lock Ari now as *this written concept plus one rough sketch*. Commission the full illustration set and any animation only once the product is validated — that's a several-thousand-euro, multi-week investment best spent after you know people are using the app.

### Ari's personality (your four qualities, made concrete)

- **Friendly** — warm, never corporate.
- **Smart** — knows the system cold and shows it casually.
- **Problem-solver** — turns confusion into a next step.
- **Guide** — and crucially, walks *beside* you, not ahead. Peer energy: the slightly-older student who's been through it, not an authority figure. That peer framing is what creates belonging.

### Ari's voice — sample lines

> "Welcome to the Agora. Let's find your place."

> "You've still got 87 of your 140 days left this year — plenty of runway. Let's find something good."

> "This one's a real fit: Python ✓, German B1 is fine here ✓, 18 hours a week ✓. Want me to write the application?"

That second line is the whole brand in one sentence — friendly, smart, problem-solving, and quietly proving the Werkstudent knowledge no competitor has.

### Where Ari shows up

- **Onboarding:** Ari greets every new user — "Welcome to the Agora. Let's find your place."
- **Empty states & loading:** Ari fills the quiet moments with a tip or encouragement.
- **Interview-prep module:** Ari becomes your coach.
- **The "Welcome to Berlin" arrival brochure:** Ari on the cover (see §4).
- **Social & video:** short, ZooZoo-style spots — Ari explaining the 140-day rule, Ari reacting to a "spray-and-pray" horror story, Ari celebrating a first interview.

### Note: Ari vs. human campus ambassadors

Ari is the **brand** ambassador (the character). Separately, you may recruit **student campus ambassadors** — real students at each university who spread Agora in their communities for perks. Don't confuse the two: Ari is the consistent face everywhere; campus ambassadors are a distribution tactic (covered in §4).

## 4. The marketing plan, step by step

Sequenced to match the project roadmap. Each phase has concrete tactics. The golden rule running through all of it: **lead with "we understand your visa," not generic "AI."**

### Phase 0 — Foundations (before any spend)

1. **Lock the brand assets.** Name (Agora), tagline (*Find your place*), Ari concept — done. Secure matching domain + social handles so they're consistent (e.g. `agora.berlin` + `@joinagora` / `@agora.berlin`). Grab the handles even before you finalize the domain.
2. **Run the trademark check** — the real gate. EUIPO TMview + German DPMA register, **class 35** (employment/recruitment) and **class 9** (software). A ~€150 Markenanwalt consult confirms it.
3. **Set up the build-log accounts:** LinkedIn (founder + an Agora company page) and X. This is your earliest, cheapest marketing.
4. **Define the voice** (see §6) and write the messaging bank (§5) so everyone on the team says the same thing.
5. **Stand up tracking:** a Notion/Airtable CRM for customer conversations; PostHog later for product analytics.

### Phase 1 — Customer development *is* marketing (weeks 1–2)

The 25 customer interviews aren't just research — they're your first seeds of word of mouth and your first design partners.

1. **25 Mom Test conversations** with international students across cohorts (Indian, Chinese, Turkish, Brazilian) and universities (TU, HU, FU, ESMT, Hertie, BIB, CODE). Ask about their last 5 applications, tools they tried, what they hated.
2. **Mine their language.** The exact words students use become your headlines. ("I never know if I'm even allowed to take a job" → that's a tagline waiting to happen.)
3. **Recruit 5–10 design partners** from these conversations into a closed beta. People who help build it become your loudest advocates.

### Phase 2 — Community-led launch (the wedge)

International students are hyper-clustered. You don't need broad reach; you need to be *the* thing passed around in specific groups.

1. **Go where they already are:**
   - University Slack / Telegram / WhatsApp groups (per-university and per-program).
   - Country-specific student associations (Indian, Chinese, Turkish, Brazilian student orgs), plus ESN and AEGEE chapters.
   - Reddit (r/berlinsocialclub, r/germany, r/cscareerquestionsEU), Discord servers, international-student Facebook groups.
2. **The "Welcome to Berlin" arrival brochure (the Trojan Horse).** A genuinely useful arrival guide for new international students — Anmeldung, blocked account, health insurance, the 140-day rule, Mindestlohn, opening a bank account — with **Ari on the cover** and Agora featured subtly inside. Distribute through the trusted EduOptions contact and student networks. It solves real arrival pain *before* pitching the app, which builds trust first. (No MLM, no agents-recruit-agents structure — that's illegal under German/EU law and kills grant eligibility.)
3. **Referral loop.** One month free for each friend who upgrades. This is the engine for WhatsApp/Telegram virality in tight-knit international communities.
4. **Build in public.** Weekly LinkedIn + X posts: what you learned in interviews, the eval suite, Ari's design, screenshots. Caps off with the methodology blog post in month ~3: *"How we made AI-generated Werkstudent CVs that pass ATS 94% of the time vs ChatGPT's 71%."* Real numbers, honest baseline — this drives both users and AI-engineering credibility.
5. **Plan everything around the October Wintersemester spike.** Arrivals in late September/October are the single biggest acquisition window of the year. Have the brochure, the campus ambassadors, and a launch campaign ready *before* it, not during.

### Phase 3 — The ongoing channel engine

1. **Organic social, Ari-led.** Short, genuinely useful explainer content — "the 140-day rule in 30 seconds," "Werkstudent vs Minijob: what's the difference," "what salary can you actually ask for" — voiced by Ari. This is content ChatGPT can't do well *and* that doubles as proof of your moat. Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts.
2. **ASO + SEO.** App Store subtitle and keywords (Werkstudent, student jobs Berlin, international student jobs, visa job Germany). A blog targeting Werkstudent legal topics (BAföG, 140-day rule, Mindestlohn, social-insurance thresholds) to win organic search — these are high-pain, low-competition queries.
3. **University partnerships.** Career centers (build on the Stellenticket relationship), international offices, and student orgs. Aim for exclusive or featured listings to improve job-supply density.
4. **Campus ambassadors (human).** One or two trusted students per university, given perks (free Pro, swag, referral bonuses) to run Agora's presence in their groups. They localize the message per community.
5. **Lifecycle & retention.** The pipeline tracker, smart follow-ups, and interview prep are themselves marketing — they create the "Agora got me the interview" stories that spread.

### Phase 4 — Paid (only after organic proves out)

1. Start small: Meta/Instagram targeting international students in Berlin; Google Search on Werkstudent terms.
2. Keep spend tied to proven unit economics. (Remember: the PWA avoids the 15–30% App Store / Play Store cut — a structural margin advantage to protect.)

### Measurement

- **North-star metric:** interviews landed per active user (the real outcome) — or, earlier in the funnel, *activation* = first tailored application sent.
- **Track to conversion target ~3.5%** free→paid, and watch retention by channel (which sources produce users who *stay*).
- **Double down on October.** Year-on-year, that window can rival a whole year's other months combined. Optimize relentlessly for it.

## 6. Brand guardrails — do's and don'ts

**DO**
- Lead with **"understands your visa / built for your situation,"** not generic "AI-powered."
- Let the moat be **audible** — drop *140-day rule*, *BAföG*, *Mindestlohn*, *Minijob* casually, the way a knowledgeable German friend would.
- Keep **Ari a peer**, never an authority figure, and always demographically open.
- Keep the tone **warm, honest, and trustworthy** — you're asking students to share CV and visa data; every signal must say "safe."
- Use the **two-tier structure** everywhere: evocative tagline on top (*Find your place*), specific subline underneath.

**DON'T**
- **Don't** market the Magic Pre-fill (Mode 2) as "AI applies for you." Frame it as **"smart autofill that saves you time"** — the student always clicks the company's own submit button. This keeps you legally clean.
- **Don't** make scammy or absolute claims ("guaranteed job"). Trust is the whole game with this audience.
- **Don't** commission expensive Ari art/animation before the product is validated. Concept + one sketch now; production later.
- **Don't** use any MLM / agents-recruit-agents distribution. It's illegal under German UWG/EU law and would kill BSS eligibility.
- **Don't** over-promise on autonomy or dilute the "belonging" core by drifting into generic "job board" language.
